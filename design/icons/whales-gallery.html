The honest test

Each concept below sits in a full-fidelity nav bar twice: once with Whales active (navy, indicator bar overhead) and once with Whales inactive (muted, 90% opacity, Stocks holding the indicator). The inactive state is the one that matters — that is where a weak mark stops being a whale and starts being a smudge. Bars sit on the page background, not on a card, so the translucent chrome reads honestly.

Concept 01

Whale Filled

The literal mark: a side-profile whale with a rounded back, a swept-back pectoral fin and a curved two-lobe fluke. A cut-out eye fixes the reading direction.

RiskAt nav size this reads as a generic fish, not specifically a whale — the two-lobe fluke is the only thing separating it from a goldfish, and the pectoral fin sits exactly where a fish’s ventral fin would. The eye cut-out is 1.75 px wide and closes up on a 1× display.

Concept 02

Surfacing fin Stroke 2

A dorsal fin cresting a full-width waterline, with the submerged body implied by a shorter arc trailing behind it. The most restrained option and the most native to the set's stroke language.

RiskA triangle above a line with an arc below is a sailboat before it is a whale. Offsetting the arc to the right fights that, but the read stays fragile. The fin’s interior also fills solid at 21 px, so it is a filled mark wearing a stroke costume.

Fluke Filled + stroke

Just the tail, mid-dive: two broad lobes with upswept tips, a deep central notch, a stock entering a stroked waterline. The single most recognisable whale shape there is — no body needed.

Concept 06

Iceberg Stroke + filled peak

A solid peak above a full-width waterline and a larger hollow mass below it. The fill/stroke contrast does the arguing: what a 13F shows you is a fraction of the position.

RiskReads as an hourglass before it reads as an iceberg — and an iceberg is not a whale. The waterline clears the underwater V by only ~1.8 units per side, so the “full-width surface” idea barely survives at nav size.

Concept 10

Fluke candle Stroke 2

A single candlestick whose lower wick forks into a whale tail. The most brand-able mark in the file — market language and whale language folded into one glyph.

The standalone whale-1.svg … whale-10.svg files in this folder are the source of truth. The markup above is an inline copy of the same path data, byte-for-byte — icons referenced through <img src> cannot inherit currentColor and would render black in the nav bar, so every mark here is drawn from an inline <symbol> sprite instead.
All ten are 24 × 24, single-path-family, currentColor only — no gradients, no second colour, no hard-coded hex. Stroked marks use width 2 with round caps and joins, matching Stocks and Watchlist; filled marks use solid silhouettes, matching Best and New.
Design mockup only. Nothing in src/ is wired to these files.
